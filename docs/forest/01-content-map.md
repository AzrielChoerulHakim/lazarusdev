## 2. Narrative premise and information architecture

**Premise:** A strange idea enters a forest. Following the path reveals the method, the clearing displays work, the river reveals curiosity, and the house invites a real conversation. The forest is navigation and editorial metaphor; it is NOT a game users must finish.

**One-line value proposition (draft):** "Lazarus Dev designs and engineers distinctive websites, interactive worlds, and digital products."

**Master editorial pair:**
- Display line: "Different worlds. Deliberate work."
- Supporting line: "Independent creative development for ideas that should not look like everything else."

### Conventional nav and anchor fallbacks
| Primary navigation label | Anchor | Information job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | #entrance | Who Lazarus is and what it offers |
| Approach | #winding-path | How the studio thinks and what it makes |
| Work | #work-clearing | Verifiable work versus clearly labeled concepts |
| Field Notes | #river-stories | Experimentation, thinking and production observations |

All chapter headings and body text are real HTML in future stages, not baked into artwork. On mobile and reduced motion, links above must work exactly like ordinary anchor links, including keyboard access. A persistent "Skip to work" and "Contact" path is recommended; no navigation depends on the forest animation.

## 7. Non-generic and Apple-inspired copy gate

- Each chapter answers a different user question (who? how? proof? thinking? next step?).
- Every display headline has supporting literal copy. No essential information is hidden in illustration.
- Show Work and Contact early in nav; don't force the user to finish scrollytelling.
- Label speculative work *at item level* and as a section disclaimer.
- Avoid identical layouts/CTA language for every scene; let chapter-specific structure dictate art.
- No user action claims success until it really occurs; copied != sent; viewed != published.
- Test future web UI for clear hierarchy, direct anchors, keyboard operation, readable text and meaningful link destinations. **None of those future runtime tests are claimed complete in Stage 01.**

## 8. Questions BLOCKING publicity, not blocking Stage 01

1. Verified contact channel (email, form backend, or external channel). Until supplied: copy-only brief with disclosure.
2. Verified published client projects and permission to showcase them. Until supplied: display three concepts as concepts.
3. Actual journal articles. Until written and linked: subjects remain internal outlines.
4. If product names or launches exist, obtain proof/URLs. Until then: describe owned products as a studio direction, not shipped inventory.
